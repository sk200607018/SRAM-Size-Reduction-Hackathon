# SRAM Cell Area Scaling Challenge

## Project Overview

This project was developed as part of a VLSI hackathon based on a problem statement provided by IISc Bangalore and Lam Research. The challenge focused on reducing the area of a baseline 6T SRAM bitcell while maintaining manufacturability under a fixed lithography resolution of 22 nm.

## Problem Statement

The objective was to achieve a 15–20% reduction in SRAM cell area while preserving the baseline fin count and satisfying the specified manufacturing constraints.

## Approach

The study explored four parameters:

* Active area dimensions
* Contact locations
* Local Interconnect (LI) routing
* Poly pitch

Different scaling attempts were evaluated using a representative BB2 half-cell layout, with layout and process information cross-analyzed to assess the results.

## Final Result

The combined scaling approach, applying 10% scaling to the four selected parameters, achieved a **19.58% reduction in BB2 cell area**, meeting the target range of 15–20% while preserving the baseline fin count.

## Key Learning Outcomes

* Gained practical exposure to SRAM bitcell architecture and layout.
* Explored layout scaling techniques and manufacturability constraints.
* Learned to evaluate individual design parameters and their combined impact on cell area.
* Improved understanding of VLSI layout analysis and optimization.

## Project Summary

The project demonstrated how coordinated scaling of active area dimensions, contact locations, local interconnect routing, and poly pitch can reduce SRAM cell area while preserving the baseline fin count. The final approach achieved a 19.58% area reduction.
